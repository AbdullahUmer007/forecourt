**2026-08-01 · Product name "Forecourt" as a working name.** UK trade term, available-sounding, not yet trade-mark checked. Reversible — flagged to Abdullah as an action.

**2026-08-01 · Stack: TypeScript / Next.js App Router / PostgreSQL with forced RLS / Drizzle / Redis+BullMQ / Cloudflare R2 / Stripe Connect.** One language across app, API and workers for a small team; Postgres RLS is the isolation mechanism; R2 chosen over S3 specifically for zero egress on vehicle photography, which is our dominant bandwidth cost. Reversible early, expensive later.

**2026-08-01 · Shared database, shared schema, `tenant_id` on every row, four isolation layers.** Right trade-off at thousands of tenants with tens of thousands of vehicles each. Schema-per-tenant doesn't scale to thousands of migrations; database-per-tenant is over-engineered. The application never assumes co-location, so a large customer can be moved to a dedicated database later. Reversible with effort.

**2026-08-01 · Money as `bigint` minor units with an explicit currency; no floats anywhere.** Property-tested. Not reversible in practice — everything downstream depends on it.

**2026-08-01 · All regulatory values live in a `compliance_rules` table keyed on the relevant date, never as constants.** UK motor finance regulation moved twice during research (AML threshold to sterling on 30 June 2026; redress scheme partially suspended in July 2026). Changing the law must be a data deployment. Not reversible — it's the whole point.

**2026-08-01 · Chart palette adopted from a validated reference palette rather than derived from the brand hue.** It passes the lightness band, chroma floor, colour-vision-deficiency separation, normal-vision separation and contrast against our exact surfaces (#FFFFFF, #16181D). Deriving a palette from "Petrol" would have needed re-validation with no user benefit. Reversible only by re-running the validator.

**2026-08-01 · Brand primary "Petrol" #0E5A6B rather than a conventional dealer blue.** 7.80:1 on white, distinctive in a category of generic blues, reads premium and mechanical. Reversible — it's a token.

**2026-08-01 · Price ABOVE the direct competitor, not below.** DealersHub is £120+VAT; our Pro tier is £189–£319 stock-banded. Undercutting a one-person operation in a commoditised tier signals a worse product and starts a race we cannot win. We sell the gap, not the price. Reversible, but strategically load-bearing.

**2026-08-01 · Build the free Dealer Site Audit tool BEFORE the CRM.** It generates pipeline before the product exists, proves expertise, doubles as the product demo and becomes our own CI regression suite. Reversible, but it would waste the strongest asset we have.

**2026-08-01 · Never name the competitor in public-facing material; never use their tenure discrepancy as an attack line.** Legally risky, makes us the aggressor, and moves the conversation away from the dealer's own broken pages. Compete instead on the verifiable trust signals they structurally lack. Not reversible without my objection on the record.

**2026-08-01 · Crawler ethics fixed: identified user agent with a contact URL, robots.txt respected, rate-limited to ~1 request/700ms, public pages only, no personal data stored, removal requests honoured immediately.** We are selling compliance; we behave accordingly. Not reversible.

**2026-08-01 · Kennington's real public data used for the demo build, marked "Demo — unsolicited".** Agreed with Abdullah. No contact made, no private data used, public sources only.

**2026-08-01 · `users` is global, not tenant-scoped.** One person may work for two dealers and an external accountant may serve several, so the tenant boundary is the membership rather than the user. `users` gets a membership-scoped RLS policy instead of a `tenant_id`. Reversible with effort; would require rewriting every membership lookup.

**2026-08-01 · `domains.hostname` is globally unique — the one deliberate exception to tenant-scoped uniqueness.** A hostname can only ever resolve to a single tenant, and an unknown or unverified host must 404 rather than fall through to a default. Not reversible: the alternative is a routing ambiguity.

**2026-08-01 · `site_scope` RLS policies are RESTRICTIVE, permanently.** Postgres combines multiple permissive policies with OR, so a permissive `site_scope` returned true whenever `scope_all_sites` was set and OR'd away `tenant_isolation` entirely on every table carrying a `site_id`. This leaked `user_sites` and the whole `audit_events` trail across tenants. Found by the hardened isolation suite. Not reversible — reverting reintroduces a cross-tenant data leak.

**2026-08-01 · The isolation suite asserts SQLSTATE 42501 on rejected cross-tenant INSERTs, and asserts rival data exists before testing against it.** A test that fails on NOT NULL rather than on the policy would pass with RLS switched off; a test with no rival rows passes vacuously. Both were true before this change. Not reversible.

**2026-08-01 · Each tenant is seeded its own copy of the nine system roles rather than sharing platform-level rows.** Lets a dealer rename and adjust roles without affecting anyone else. Costs nine rows per tenant, which is nothing. Reversible.

**2026-08-01 · The compliance profile is enforced in three places** — TypeScript validation for the good error message, database CHECK constraints for the guarantee, and the go-live checklist for operational readiness. Deliberate duplication: the database is the guarantee, the code is the explanation. Reversible but inadvisable.

**2026-08-02 · Vehicle registration is `UNIQUE (tenant_id, registration)`, never global.** A global unique index would let one dealer discover another's stock: enter a registration, get a constraint violation, and you have learned a competitor holds that car. Verified behaviourally — two tenants can both hold WN22HNL, one tenant cannot duplicate it. Note this is the OPPOSITE conclusion to `domains.hostname`, where global uniqueness is the safe choice because a hostname can only resolve to one tenant. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · `delivered` is NOT a terminal vehicle state.** A customer has a 30-day short-term right to reject under CRA s.22, so a delivered vehicle can come back. Modelling delivery as terminal would leave no lawful path to record a rejection — the exact scenario the Deal Evidence Ledger exists for. Caught by a reachability test. Reversible only by removing the CRA rejection path, which would be wrong.

**2026-08-02 · Days-to-sell is measured from Live, not from purchase.** Time before Live is `daysToLive` and belongs to the prep team. Blending them blames the sales team for time the car spent in the workshop, and a dealer who spots that stops trusting the whole dashboard. Reversible but inadvisable.

**2026-08-02 · Go-live blockers are returned as a list with per-blocker `overridable` flags, not a boolean.** The dealer needs to see exactly what to fix. An incomplete VAT stock book, a missing price and an unacknowledged mileage anomaly are non-overridable; a missing provenance check is overridable with a recorded reason and a named authoriser. Reversible.

**2026-08-02 · M4 was split into a free half and a paid half, and the free half was built immediately.** DVLA VES and DVSA MOT History are free and self-serve; only valuation (cap hpi) and provenance (HPI Check) need commercial contracts. Labelling the whole module "blocked on contracts" was wrong and would have idled work for weeks. The free half also populates `vehicles.highest_mot_mileage`, which M3's go-live gate already reads — that gate was guarding against data nothing produced. Reversible: the paid adapters slot into the same interface.

**2026-08-02 · Adapters are tested against recorded fixtures, never a live provider.** No contract, no network, no flakiness, and provider behaviour (retries, circuit breaking, partial failure) becomes deterministic. A nightly job should later compare live responses against fixture shape and alert on drift — these APIs change without notice. Reversible.

**2026-08-02 · A combined vehicle lookup returns partial data rather than failing.** If MOT history is down we still return the DVLA record, with the failed source NAMED in a `degraded` array. A buyer standing in an auction hall would rather have half the car than an error. Reversible.

**2026-08-02 · Mileage anomaly detection reports EVERY reading below the running highest, not just the first.** Reporting only the first makes a clocked car look like a single clerical error. Caught by my own test expecting one anomaly where the data honestly contained two. Not reversible without weakening a fraud signal.

**2026-08-02 · EXIF stripping is mandatory and unconditional, enforced by a CHECK constraint.** A phone photo carries GPS. Publishing it discloses where the dealer's stock sits overnight and — for a part-exchange appraisal — usually a customer's home address. `vehicle_media_published_requires_ready` makes it impossible to publish an image that has not been stripped. Not reversible: it is a data-protection control, not an optimisation.

**2026-08-02 · Damage photographs shown to a buyer cannot be deleted, only unpublished.** A photograph of declared condition, shown before sale, is a Consumer Rights Act defence. Enforced by a CHECK constraint as well as in domain logic, and the refusal message offers the lawful alternative rather than just saying no. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · Damage photographs sort to the END of the published set.** They must be present and visible — that is the defence — but leading a listing with a scuffed bumper loses the click the defence was meant to protect. Reversible; revisit if dealers ask for it.

**2026-08-02 · `vehicles.published_photo_count` is maintained by a database trigger, not application code.** M3's go-live gate reads it, so a missed update would silently block or unblock a vehicle from being advertised. The database owns anything a gate depends on. Reversible but inadvisable.

**2026-08-02 · Uploads are validated by magic bytes, not the content-type header.** The header is caller-supplied and therefore untrustworthy. HEIC and AVIF are exempted from sniffing because they share an ISO-BMFF container signature; the processor rejects them later if they turn out not to be images. Reversible.

**2026-08-02 · A sold vehicle 301s to the closest-priced car of the same model, never to a "Sold Out" page.** Those pages accumulate as near-identical dead ends, waste crawl budget, compete with each other, and drop a real buyer who clicked through from search onto nothing. Verified by property test that every sold URL resolves to a 301 with a destination. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · Facet landing pages always render but only index above a stock threshold.** Two failure modes, both observed on the competitor's estate: pages indexed by Google that 404, and thin pages with no stock. Rendering always (never 404 a URL we minted) with `noindex, follow` below three matching vehicles solves both. Reversible; the threshold is a parameter.

**2026-08-02 · Structured data must never contain a finance figure.** A monthly payment or APR in JSON-LD is still a financial promotion under CONC 3.5.3R, and JSON-LD has nowhere to carry the representative example that must accompany it. `assertNoFinanceFigures` enforces this and is asserted in tests. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · `aggregateRating` is omitted unless there is a genuine rating with a non-zero review count.** An invented or zero-count rating is a structured-data violation and can earn a manual penalty. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · We audit our own generated output with our own tool, as a blocking test.** We sell by auditing competitors' customers' sites; if ours failed the same checks the pitch collapses, and the first dealer to run the free tool on us would find out. `tests/self-audit/generated-site.test.ts` scores ≥85 today, with the finance check deliberately failing until M8 and asserted as a known gap so it flips when M8 lands. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · The public site's markup is generated by template functions, not React components.** The VDP must render completely without JavaScript and ship under 120KB of it. React server components would satisfy that today, but template functions make it impossible to regress: no client bundle to grow, no `use client` to slip in during a refactor, no hydration cost. The Next.js routes are thin wrappers that call the render layer and return the string. Interactivity that genuinely needs JS is added as separately-budgeted progressive enhancement. Reversible, but the zero-JS test would have to be weakened to do it.

**2026-08-02 · An unknown or unverified host returns 404 and never falls through to a default tenant.** Falling through would serve one dealer's stock under another dealer's domain. Unverified matters separately: anyone can point a CNAME at us, and serving before the TXT challenge passes would let them impersonate a dealer on a domain they do not control. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · Every cache key carries the tenant, enforced by the `cacheKey` helper's signature.** A cache key without a tenant serves the previous tenant's rendered HTML to the next request — a leak that bypasses the database entirely, where no RLS policy can catch it. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · The self-audit now runs against real renderer output rather than a hand-built fixture.** A fixture drifts from the renderer silently; real output cannot. Writing it caught a genuine harness error — I had passed the VDP as the home page, so the `vehicle-page-titles` check failed exactly as designed. The check was right and the test was wrong, which is the correct way round. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · The Claude Design VDP was reviewed against our own renderer, and where it was better we changed ours.** It used our tokens exactly, so the comparison was purely about substance. Six things it did better and we have adopted: a real GB plate (yellow field, blue UK band) instead of a flat yellow chip; a labelled "Cash price" with the reduction from our own price history beside it; provenance stated as an outcome rather than a bare badge; declared marks counted and *named*, not just photographed; EV battery health given its typical range for context; and mileage drawn as a chart above the MOT table rather than the table alone. The review was worth more than the design: three of the six are data-model changes, not styling.

**2026-08-02 · We did NOT adopt three things from the design, and each omission is deliberate.** The market guide-price comparison ("priced £651 under guide") needs cap hpi valuations, which are contract-blocked — publishing a comparison we cannot evidence is the exact behaviour we audit competitors for, and there is now a test asserting the page makes no guide-price claim. The advisory-to-prep follow-through ("both tyres replaced in prep on 22 Jul, receipt in the history") needs M14's prep records. The finance block is M8: `financeHtml` stays `null` and no payment figure can appear. The design validated all three as targets; none of them ships on a promise.

**2026-08-02 · Provenance fields are tri-state, and an unknown is never rendered as a clear.** `outstandingFinance`, `stolen` and `writtenOff` are each `true | false | null`. Only an explicit `false` produces "no outstanding finance"; `null` produces silence. Rendering a missing field as good news is how a provenance badge becomes a misrepresentation. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · An adverse provenance result is disclosed on the public page, not suppressed.** A vehicle with recorded outstanding finance renders "Provenance — declared" with what was found, and cannot render "Provenance clear". A dealer who wants to hide it is the dealer we are selling against, and it is a Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations problem as well as a moral one. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · The mileage chart's y-axis starts at zero, and no chart is drawn from a single reading.** A truncated axis exaggerates every step on a chart whose entire job is to be trusted, and a one-point "trend" is a lie with a line through it. Both are asserted by test. The MOT table below is the chart's table view, satisfying the design-system rule. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · The provenance badge was removed from the trust block and now appears once, above the fold.** It was rendering twice — once as a fact and once as a badge further down. Saying it in two places made neither instance read as a fact. Reversible.

**2026-08-02 · Faceted search is crawl-controlled by an allow-list, not by hope.** Ten facets with five values each is 9.7 million URLs of near-identical content, all competing with each other and with the vehicle pages that actually sell cars. It is the most common way a dealer site quietly destroys its own organic traffic, and it is invisible until it has already happened. Only a small set of shapes may be indexed — all stock, one make, one make plus one model, and any of those plus ONE band-aligned refinement — and every link into anything else carries `rel="nofollow"`. A test enumerates 720 realistic URL combinations and asserts that 26 are indexable. The buyer still gets every filter; only crawlers are constrained. Not reversible without reopening the crawl space.

**2026-08-02 · Only fuel, transmission and body style may refine an indexable URL.** Colour, doors and seats are how a buyer narrows a list once they are already on the site; they are not how anyone arrives from Google. "Used electric BMW" is a search with intent behind it, "used white BMW" is not, and indexing it would add twenty-five thin pages per make. Caught by my own test, which asserted a colour link would be nofollowed when the rule as written would have indexed it — the test was right about the intent and the rule was too permissive. Reversible; the set is a constant.

**2026-08-02 · The canonical URL drops the sort order and keeps the page number.** Sorting reorders a set without changing it, so every sort of one filter combination is duplicate content and canonicalises to the unsorted URL. The page number is kept because canonicalising page 2 to page 1 tells Google page 2 does not exist — a well-known way to lose deep listings. Page 2 and beyond is `noindex, follow`: nothing is orphaned, because every vehicle has its own URL in the sitemap. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · Free-form price and mileage ranges are never indexed or followed.** `?price_max=9999` and `?price_max=10000` are two URLs showing almost the same cars, and the space is unbounded. A buyer may still type any number; only the nine declared price bands and six mileage bands are crawlable. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · Unknown query parameters are dropped, not passed through.** A marketplace or ad platform appends its own tracking parameters; passing them through would fork the edge cache and mint a new indexable URL per click. `parseSearchQuery` returns the list it ignored so a test can prove nothing silently widened the crawl space. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · A zero-count facet option is shown, counted and disabled — never hidden.** A sidebar whose options appear and vanish as you filter is impossible to use, and a buyer cannot tell "there are none of these" from "I have mis-filtered". A selected option stays clickable even at zero, or there is no way to undo it. Reversible.

**2026-08-02 · Monthly-payment search is blocked by an assertion, not merely unbuilt.** It is a large conversion win and it is the single most dangerous facet on the site: a payment figure is a financial promotion under CONC 3.5.3R and the search grid has nowhere to put the representative example. `assertNoPaymentFilter` throws if `monthly`, `ppm`, `payment`, `per_month` or `apr` ever appears in a URL, so adding the facet without `<FinancePromotion>` fails loudly at the boundary instead of shipping a payment nobody signed off. Not reversible before M8 and adviser sign-off.

**2026-08-02 · A zero-result page relaxes the search and shows the closest real cars.** The ladder drops the least important constraint first — colour, then doors and seats, then mileage, then ±10% on budget (roughly a dealer's negotiating room), then gearbox, fuel, model, keyword, and the make last — and stops at the first step with stock. A zero-result page is where a dealer loses a buyer who was ready to spend; an apology is the worst possible thing to put there. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · A search that finds nothing is recorded as a demand signal, in an append-only partitioned table.** "Eleven people looked for an automatic Qashqai under £12,000 last month and we had none" is a buying instruction, and it is the reason the zero-result page exists at all. Only thin and empty results are recorded — logging every search would bury the signal and store far more visitor behaviour than we need — and what is stored is the normalised query, never the raw URL, so two spellings of one search aggregate together. Not reversible: a table that can be edited after the fact is not evidence of anything.

**2026-08-02 · The shortlist cookie is set on the first SAVE, never on page load.** That is the entire basis of its PECR "strictly necessary" exemption: it serves a service the user explicitly requested by clicking save. Setting it on arrival would make it a consent-requiring cookie and the exemption would be a fiction. `shouldIssueToken` is a function with a test rather than a comment in a route handler. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · A shortlist token is treated as a credential: 32 random bytes, unique per TENANT, never global.** It is a list of what a buyer can afford and when they were looking. Uniqueness is `(tenant_id, token)` rather than `(token)`, because a global unique index would leak the existence of another dealer's token through a conflict — and the isolation suite now asserts both the index shape and that another dealer's token resolves to nothing. A cross-tenant shortlist merge throws rather than returning a wrong answer. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · A car that sells stays on the shortlist, marked, with a replacement link.** Deleting it silently makes the buyer think we lost it, and wastes the one moment we know exactly what they wanted. Merging two shortlists keeps the EARLIEST save date, because the CRM shows "saved 11 days ago" and resetting it would misrepresent how long that buyer has been deciding. Reversible.

**2026-08-02 · Saved-search alerts cannot send without an M9 consent record, re-checked at send time.** `canSendAlert` returns false with a stated reason until M9 lands, and thereafter re-tests consent, suppression, whether anything actually matches, and the frequency gap at the moment of sending rather than at the moment of scheduling. A search saved in March and sent in June must be tested against a withdrawal made in May. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · The isolation suite's structural gate was exempting every partitioned table.** Gate 1 filtered on `relkind = 'r'`, so a partitioned parent — `search_events` now, `audit_events` and `vehicle_views` later — was never checked for RLS at all. Same blind spot that was found and fixed in `verify-policies.mjs` on 2 August; it had survived in the test. Found by adding the first partitioned table since. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · We verify finance quotes; we do not originate them.** `calculations.md` §7 settled this in the planning phase and building M8 confirmed it was right: the finance platform or the lender calculates the payment and the APR and carries the regulatory responsibility for that calculation. What we added is the other half — `verifyQuote` re-derives the amount of credit, the total charge for credit, the total amount payable and the APR itself from the cashflows, and refuses to display anything that does not reconcile. "The provider sent it" is not a defence: the instant we render a figure it is our financial promotion, and the lender's arithmetic error becomes our breach. APR is solved by bisection on the Consumer Credit Directive equation rather than Newton–Raphson, because bisection cannot diverge or land on a spurious root and speed is irrelevant here. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · A cost-of-credit figure cannot be rendered without an `ApprovedPromotion`, a type only the gate can construct.** The build plan's acceptance criterion was "there is no code path, in any renderer, that produces a bare payment figure". That is now enforced by the signature: `renderFinancePromotion` takes an `ApprovedPromotion`, and only `approvePromotion()` returns one, and it throws unless the rule is signed off, the example is approved, in date, and arithmetically sound. The vehicle page takes the same token rather than pre-rendered HTML, so it is not possible to hand the page a payment that did not come with its example. The monthly-payment search facet M7 blocked is unlocked by the same proof and nothing else. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · The representative example, its field list, its ORDER, its prominence rule and the 51% threshold are versioned data, not code.** CONC 3.5 is being consulted on right now — CP26/15 opened 29 April 2026, closed 17 June 2026, and asks whether the mandatory representative example supports consumer understanding at all and whether 51% is still the right threshold. No policy statement had been published as at 2 August 2026, so the current rules stand, but they may not stand for long. When the FCA moves we ship a signed-off row in `compliance_rules`, not a release. Crucially this also changes what the compliance consultant reviews: a data record with a source URL, rather than a diff. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · The seeded compliance rule ships UNSIGNED, and nothing renders until a named person signs it.** `signed_off_by` and `signed_off_at` are null in the migration, `validateRepresentativeExample` fails on an unsigned rule, and an isolation test asserts that no migration ever ships a pre-signed rule. This is the launch gate for M8, M11 and M12, and it is impossible to forget because the feature simply does not work without it. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · M1's seeded CONC rule had the representative-example fields in the wrong order, and v1 was left in place anyway.** CONC 3.5.5R prescribes a sequence, and the order is part of the rule rather than a presentation choice; M1's seed also split "rate is fixed" into its own field where the rule treats the rate and its fixed/variable nature as one item. v2 corrects both. v1 was NOT edited or closed — it is the record of what we believed on 1 August, and rewriting history in a compliance table is precisely what that table exists to prevent. The resolver therefore prefers the highest version whose window covers the date, so overlapping open windows are normal and expected. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · `compliance_rules` has had no table grant since M1 created it.** `apply_tenant_policies()` issues privileges only to tables carrying a `tenant_id`, and `compliance_rules` deliberately has none — so the application role could never have read the VAT fraction, the AML threshold or the CRA windows it is supposed to read from there. Nothing had exercised it yet. Found by the isolation test written for M8, fixed in `0006_finance.sql` with an explicit grant, plus RLS making it readable by every tenant and writable by none: a tenant able to edit its own representative-example rule could switch its own compliance gate off, which is worse than any single bad promotion.

**2026-08-02 · Cost-of-credit language in free text blocks publishing.** The component gate protects figures the code puts on the page; the realistic breach is a dealer typing "Only £199 a month!" into a description, a caption or a page title, where no component is involved. The scanner is anchored on the credit signal — a period, a rate, an APR, a credit word — and never on the presence of a number, because a scanner that flags every cash price gets switched off within a week and then protects nothing. With a valid example on the page the findings are reported but do not block, because a payment typed into prose cannot follow the lender's rates when they move. Reversible; the patterns are data-shaped.

**2026-08-02 · The representative APR is governed monthly against what actually completed, and a small sample is never reported as a pass.** "Representative" means at least 51% of business resulting from the promotion is expected to be concluded at that rate or better, and the only honest evidence for that expectation is concluded agreements. A drifted representative APR is a live breach on every page of a dealer's website simultaneously and nobody notices until a complaint arrives — which is the exact shape of the problem the industry is currently paying about £9bn for. Below 20 agreements the report says "not a pass, there is simply not enough evidence yet" rather than showing a comfortable percentage. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · The payment figure sits above the representative example on the page, never the reverse, and the APR is rendered larger than the payment.** CONC 3.5.6R requires the representative APR to be given greater prominence than any other cost-of-credit figure in the promotion, including the payment that triggered it. That is enforced twice: exactly one item may carry `fp-prominent` and it must be the one the rule names, and a test parses the actual CSS to assert the prominent font size exceeds the payment's. A prominence rule that lives only in someone's intention survives exactly one CSS refactor. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · A discretionary commission arrangement cannot be recorded at all.** Banned since January 2021. Modelling it — even to store a historical one — would imply it were an option, and the commission-disclosure validator refuses the type outright. Commission disclosure requires the amount or its basis AND the exact wording version shown, because a disclosure nobody can reproduce is not evidence, which is the finding at the centre of the post-*Hopcraft* liability. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · The self-audit gap is closed: our generated site now scores 100 out of 100 on our own tool.** `finance-display` is the heaviest check in the audit at 18 points and was deliberately left failing with a written note that it would start failing the moment M8 landed. It did, and it has been flipped to assert a pass plus a whole-audit score of exactly 100 with no check outstanding. The competitor's live customer site scores 16. That number is now safe to put in front of a dealer.

**2026-08-02 · "Total amount payable" is the statutory figure and excludes the deposit — we had it wrong, and a competitor's page found it.** s.189 CCA 1974 and the Consumer Credit (Disclosure of Information) Regulations 2010 define it as the total amount of credit plus the total charge for credit. We had it as total cash outlay, which reads more naturally in English and is not what the term means. Found by running Kennington's published representative example through `verifyQuote`: every one of their figures reconciled on the statutory definition and failed on ours. Their example is now a permanent regression test. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · Kennington's advertised representative APR appears to be their interest rate.** Their published example — £45,999 cash price, £5,999 deposit, £40,000 credit, 48 × £993.50 plus a final £496.75, total charge for credit £8,184.75 — reconciles exactly, but £993.50 is precisely the payment for 8.90% as a NOMINAL annual rate compounded monthly. The APR implied by the same cashflows is 9.8%, and CONC App 1.2 requires the APR to be an effective annual rate. Recorded in `demo/kennington.ts` with the arithmetic. **Not to be used in any customer-facing document until the retained FCA compliance consultant confirms the reading** — being wrong about a competitor's compliance in a pitch would be worse than not raising it.

**2026-08-02 · The demo uses Kennington's real business facts and invented stock.** Their name, address, telephone numbers, opening hours and FRN are public facts about a business we are pitching, and a demo that shows a different dealer's details is a worse demo. Their photographs and listing copy are copyrighted, and a substantial extraction of their 123 listings would also engage the UK sui generis database right — so the stock is invented, shaped to their actual size and price spread, with obviously-generated placeholder photographs. `demo/kennington-stock.json` overrides it if a real scrape is ever taken. Reversible, but the IP position is not.

**2026-08-02 · The public site is route handlers, not page components.** The renderers return complete HTML documents; a React page would nest one document inside another and reintroduce the client bundle the whole design exists to avoid. Next keeps doing what it is good at — routing, caching, revalidation — and nothing stands between a buyer and the markup. As a side effect `apps/site` has no React in its own code at all. Not reversible without weakening the zero-JS test.

**2026-08-02 · Tenant resolution moved out of Next middleware.** Middleware was the right place — it runs before everything, so no handler can forget — but it executes in the Edge runtime, which has no TCP sockets and therefore cannot reach Postgres, where the host-to-tenant mapping lives. The production fix is an edge-readable replica of the domain table; until that exists, `requireTenant` in `src/request.ts` is the ONLY export that hands out a tenant id, so a handler that skips it has nothing to pass to the data layer. `resolveTenant` itself is untouched and still the tested pure function that refuses an unknown or unverified host. Reversible once the edge replica exists.

**2026-08-02 · The dev seed leaves the compliance rule unsigned, and the local site therefore shows no payment.** It would have been easy to sign it in the seed so the finance block appears on a fresh checkout. That would also mean the one gate protecting the riskiest feature in the product is off in the environment where everyone works, and nobody would notice when it stopped working. `DEMO_SIGN_COMPLIANCE_RULE=1` writes rule version 999 with a sign-off explicitly labelled as not a real approval. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · `localhost` is seeded as a VERIFIED domain rather than the middleware being relaxed in development.** An unverified-host bypass for dev is a bypass that eventually ships. The dev environment exercises the same refusal production does, and the seed grants the verification the same way a real domain gets it. Not reversible.

**2026-08-02 · Colour facets fold manufacturer paint names to base colours.** "Cosmos Black", "Frozen White", "Infra Red", "Moonstone Grey" — every marque invents its own, so an un-normalised colour facet is thirty options with one car behind each, which is not a filter. `baseColour()` maps them; the full paint name still appears on the vehicle page, where it is what a buyer wants to read. Found by looking at the rendered facet list rather than by reasoning about it. Reversible; the mapping is a table.

**2026-08-02 · Result-card badges sit on the photograph, not in the flow.** With a variable number of badges between the name and the price, every card in the grid started its price at a different height and the row read as broken. Moving them onto the image and pinning the save button to the bottom of the card fixed the rhythm. Reversible.

**2026-08-03 · The public site was rebuilt from the Claude Design canvas, and the design's diagnosis was correct.** The brief's own list of what was wrong — one type size doing all the work, no masthead, photography not designed for, uniform grey cards, the finance block as a spreadsheet, trust stated rather than shown — described the build accurately. `.vdp-title` was 24px, `.vdp-price` was 28px and `.card h2` was 20px, so nothing on the page had rank; every section was `.card`. The new scale runs from 12px to a 76px display price, and three surfaces (page plane, white band, dark plane) replace the single card. Reversible, but the zero-JS and audit gates constrain any replacement the same way.

**2026-08-03 · The APR now leads the finance block visually, not merely by font size.** CONC 3.5.6R requires the representative APR to have greater prominence than any other cost-of-credit figure. The previous layout satisfied that literally — `.fp-prominent dd` was larger than `.fp-payment-amount` and a test proved it — while the payment still read as the headline, because it came first and the APR was a row inside a table. The APR is now the first thing in the block at up to 104px and the payment sits in a quieter card beneath it. The font-size test still holds; what changed is that the rule is now satisfied by structure rather than by a measurement that was technically true. Not reversible.

**2026-08-03 · Each mandated item in the representative example carries a plain-English gloss, as a SIBLING of its `<dt>`.** The example is legally required; a buyer reading it is not, and eight bare label/value rows are not read. The gloss sits after the `<dt>`/`<dd>` pair and is pulled onto its own line by flex `order`, deliberately NOT inside the `<dt>` — the label must stay byte-for-byte the rule's wording so the ordering and prominence tests can assert against it. The first attempt put the gloss inside the `<dt>` and broke the prominence test, which was right to fail. Reversible; the glosses are strings.

**2026-08-03 · Two tests had been left failing, and both were the same unfinished correction.** DECISIONS.md records on 2 August that "total amount payable" excludes the deposit, but the fix reached the production code and only some of the fixtures. `generated-site.test.ts` still added the advance payment, so `approvePromotion` correctly refused it and the whole suite — including the 100/100 self-audit assertion — failed to load and ran ZERO tests. The golden file still held the pre-correction £14,000. The gate worked exactly as designed; what failed was finishing the change. A correction to a compliance definition has to be swept through every fixture in the same commit.

**2026-08-03 · `raw()` and `when()` interpolated into a plain template literal silently delete content.** They return marker objects that only the `html` tagged template unwraps; anywhere else they stringify to `[object Object]`. `renderVehiclePage` returns a plain template literal, so during the redesign this ate the vehicle's derivative and the dealer's phone number out of the enquiry section — the highest-intent control on the page — and not one test noticed, because every test asserted on what should be PRESENT rather than on the marker. Two tests now assert the marker is absent. The real fix would be a `Raw` type that cannot stringify, but the cheap assertion catches the whole class today. Not reversible.

**2026-08-03 · The masthead phone button collapses to its icon below 480px.** At 390px the full number pushed the brand block off the edge and clipped the town out of "Open until 6pm · Bletchley, Milton Keynes". The button keeps its 44px target and carries an `aria-label` with the number, so nothing is lost to a screen reader — and the number still appears in the sticky CTA bar and the footer. Found by looking at the rendered page on a phone viewport, not by reasoning about it. Reversible.

**2026-08-03 · The home page is built, and the root no longer redirects to the stock list.** A 307 to `/used-cars` was a reasonable placeholder and a poor home page: a dealer's domain is what goes on their forecourt banner, and it landed every direct visit on a filtered list. The hero is search-first rather than a stock photograph, because these buyers arrive with a budget and a body style already in mind. "Why buy here" sits ABOVE the stock grid: a buyer deciding whether to trust this dealer should not have to scroll past a grid to find out. Every figure on it — the stock count, the lowest price, the browse-by counts — comes from live stock, so a dealer with 12 cars gets an honest page rather than one padded to look like a dealer with 300, and a test asserts the zero-stock variant does not invent any of them. Reversible.
